♠ Bhabhi Thulla

Get rid of all your cards. The last person still holding cards is the Bhabhi, and that's the one result you don't want.

1. At a glance

Players3 to 7
DeckOne standard 52-card deck, no jokers
Card orderA (highest) K Q J 10 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 (lowest)
Trump suitNone. Only the suit that was led matters.
GoalEmpty your hand. The last player with cards is the Bhabhi (loser).
ScoreboardThulla only hands out losses 💀. Nobody "wins", everyone else just escapes.

2. Setup & the deal

  • Someone creates a room with ♠ Thulla selected and shares the 4-letter code or invite link.
  • Once at least 3 people are seated, the host presses Start game.
  • All 52 cards are dealt out as evenly as possible. With 5 players, for example, some get 11 cards and some get 10.
  • Your hand is sorted by suit at the bottom of the screen. Tap a card to play it when it's your turn.

3. Trick 1: the Ace of Spades

  • Whoever holds the A♠ starts. Right after the deal, everyone sees a banner such as "Ali has the Ace of Spades" and Ali's chip glows gold. A moment later the game throws the card for them automatically, and it can't be taken back.
  • Everyone then plays one card in turn (clockwise).
  • Trick 1 is special: no matter what anyone plays, even a different suit, the trick just goes to the waste pile. Nobody picks anything up on trick 1.
  • Since the A♠ is the highest spade, its holder also leads trick 2, and that's where the real game starts.

💡 Trick 1 is a free chance to get rid of a card you don't want, because nothing bad can happen yet.

4. Playing a trick (trick 2 onward)

  1. The leader plays any card. Its suit is the led suit.
  2. Everyone after them must follow suit (play the same suit) if they have it.
  3. If everyone follows suit, it's a clean trick: the cards go to the waste pile, and whoever played the highest card of the led suit leads the next trick.
  4. If someone can't follow suit, they play any other card. That's a thulla (next section).

5. What is a "thulla"?

A thulla is an off-suit card played by someone who has run out of the led suit. It changes everything:

  • The trick ends immediately. Players after the thulla don't play to it.
  • The player who played the highest card of the led suit has to pick up the whole trick, thulla card included, into their hand.
  • That same player then leads the next trick.

⚠️ The thulla card itself doesn't count as "highest". Only cards of the led suit can be highest. So playing a big card in the led suit is risky: if someone after you throws a thulla, you're the one picking up.

6. Getting safe & the end of the game

  • When your hand is empty you're safe ("bahar"). You stop playing and watch the rest.
  • There's no drawing from the waste pile. If your last card wins a clean trick, you're still safe, and the next player who still has cards leads.
  • The game ends when only one player has cards left. That player is the Bhabhi 💀.
  • If the last players all empty their hands on the same trick, the game is a draw.
  • When it ends, the host can press Play again for a new deal. The scoreboard keeps counting.

7. Switch: Ask to take cards

This button shows up for the leader as Ask to take <name>'s cards (N).

  • When: only when it's your turn to lead, from trick 2 on, and before you play a card.
  • What: you ask to take the whole hand of the next player (clockwise) who still has cards.
  • Their choice: they see Hand them over / Decline. Nothing moves unless they accept.
  • If they accept, all their cards come to you and they are instantly safe.
  • If they decline, you can ask again later. Playing a card instead cancels the request.
  • Not allowed once only 2 players have cards left.

Why take cards? A bigger hand with more suits makes you less likely to run out of a suit, and you get to choose what to lead. It's a gamble, though, because you now have more cards to get rid of.

8. Switch: Concede 🏳️

  • When: only when exactly 2 players still have cards.
  • Either of them can press 🏳️ Concede the game when the ending is obvious, to save time.
  • The other player sees Accept the win / Keep playing.
  • If they accept, the game ends at once: the player who conceded is the Bhabhi ("game parr gayi").
  • If they decline, play continues and the offer can be made again. Playing a card cancels a pending offer.

9. Switch: Change card (undo) ↩

  • Tapped the wrong card? A ↩ Change card (10s) button appears right after you play.
  • You have about 10 seconds, and only until the next card hits the table.
  • The automatic A♠ opening can't be undone.

10. A worked example

Four players: Ali, Bilal, Saad, Hamza. It's trick 5 and Ali leads.

  1. Ali leads 9♥.
  2. Bilal follows with K♥.
  3. Saad has no hearts left, so he plays 4♣. Thulla!
  4. The trick ends right away; Hamza doesn't play. The highest heart is Bilal's K♥, so Bilal picks up all three cards (9♥, K♥, 4♣) and leads the next trick.

If Saad had a heart instead, say 3♥, and Hamza also followed, the trick would be clean. It would go to the waste pile and Bilal (K♥) would lead next without picking anything up.

11. Tips for new players

  • Keep track of which suits each player has run out of, because they're the ones who can thulla.
  • Get rid of high cards early, while everyone still has the suit and a thulla is less likely.
  • When a suit has gone round a few times, play low in it. The person with the highest card is the one who gets hit.
  • Running out of a suit yourself is good: it means you can thulla someone else.

🎺 Rung (Court Piece)

Two teams of two. Win 7 of the 13 sars for your team. This is the Pakistani double-sar version: winning a sar on its own isn't enough, you have to win twice in a row to collect.

1. At a glance

PlayersExactly 4, in 2 teams
DeckOne standard 52-card deck, 13 cards each
Card orderA (highest) K Q J 10 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 (lowest)
RungThe trump suit. Any rung card beats any card of another suit.
GoalYour team collects 7 or more of the 13 sars. Winning 13–0 is a whitewash 🧹.
ScoreboardAll four players get a 🏆 win or a 💀 loss. Whitewashes are counted separately.

2. Teams & seats

  • Seats 1 & 3 are one team, seats 2 & 4 the other. Partners sit opposite each other.
  • In the lobby or between games, the host can tap two players to swap their seats and set the teams.
  • If more than 4 people are seated, the first four play and the rest sit out that deal.
  • Only 3 of you? The host can tap 🤖 Add a bot to seat Botta (see Botta).

3. Who calls the rung

  • First game: a toss. Everyone gets one card face up, and the highest card calls the rung.
    • If players on opposite teams tie, only they toss again.
    • If both partners of the same team tie, that team has won the toss.
  • After that: the team that won the last game picks. Whichever partner taps 🎺 I'll pick the rung first becomes the caller, so sort it out on voice.
  • The room goes back to a toss when the last result doesn't apply: the last game was aborted, you played Thulla in between, a winner left, or a seat swap split the winning pair.
  • No cards are dealt until the caller is decided.

4. The deal & choosing the rung

  1. Everyone gets 5 cards.
  2. The caller looks at their 5 cards and taps a suit (♠ ♥ ♣ ♦). That suit is the rung and it's announced to everyone.
  3. The rest of the cards are dealt in two more rounds of 4, so everyone has 5 + 4 + 4 = 13.
  4. The caller leads the first sar.

💡 Caller: pick the suit you have the most of, or the strongest cards in, among your first 5.

5. Playing a sar

A sar is one round where each of the 4 players plays a card (what other games call a trick).

  1. The leader plays any card.
  2. Everyone else must follow suit if they can.
  3. If you can't follow suit, play anything, including a rung (trump) card. Using a rung card here is called cutting.
  4. The highest rung card played heads the sar. If no rung was played, the highest card of the led suit heads it.
  5. Whoever heads the sar is senior and leads the next one.

6. The pile: how sars are actually won

This is what makes double-sar different from regular Court Piece.

  • Heading a sar does not win it. Finished sars stay face up in a pile in the middle.
  • To pick up the pile, the same player must head a sar and then head the next one too, the one they lead. In other words: win, then defend your lead.
  • If anyone beats the leader, opponent or partner, the pickup fails. The pile stays, and the new senior player now has to defend in turn.
  • Sars 1–4 can never be picked up. The earliest pickup is sar 5.
  • A pickup takes the whole pile for your team. Picking up at sar 5 means 5 sars at once.

The status line tells you what's at stake, for example "Ali takes 6 if unbeaten".

7. The bar after a pickup

  • The sar right after a pickup is barred. Nobody can pick it up, and it starts the new pile.
  • The same player can still collect the next pile. For example, pick up at 5, head the barred 6, defend 7, and you take 6 + 7.

8. Sar 13 & winning the game

  • All 13 sars are always played.
  • Sar 13 is special: whoever heads it takes everything left in the pile, even without defending, even if it's barred. So the two teams' totals always add up to 13.
  • The first team to reach 7 wins. The win is announced as soon as it's certain, and the remaining sars are still played to settle the margin.
  • 13–0 is a whitewash 🧹.
  • The host presses Play again for the next deal. The deal rotates around the table.

9. House-rule switches

Before a game and on the game-over screen, three buttons above the table show which house rules are on, for example 🂡 Ace can't win the sar: ON. Everyone can see them; only the host can flip them, and only between games. The settings stay for the room. All three are ON by default. Turn them off for guests who play classic rung.

🂡 Ace can't win the sar default ON

  • If you defend your lead (try to pick up the pile) and your card heading that sar is an Ace, you stay senior but pick up nothing.
  • The pile stays, you keep the lead, and the next sar can be won as normal.
  • An Ace can still head or cut a sar. It just can't be the card that collects.
  • Sar 13 is not affected: an Ace heading sar 13 still takes everything.

OFF (classic): Aces pick up like any other card.

🂡🂡 2nd Ace in a row = 2 default ON

  • If the card you played in the previous sar was an Ace that stayed senior (it headed that sar), then an Ace you play in this sar counts as the 2 of its suit, the lowest card.
  • A demoted rung Ace is still a rung card, just the lowest one.
  • Because it's now a "2" and not an Ace, it can win the sar even with the 🂡 rule on.
  • An Ace that got cut or was thrown off-suit doesn't count: you weren't senior.
  • It works in pairs only. A third Ace in a row is a full Ace again, and any non-Ace resets it.

⚠️ It's a trap: the game doesn't warn you. You only find out when the card lands ("counts as 2"), and that play can't be undone. Remember what you played last sar!

OFF: every Ace plays at full strength.

🎺 Cannot win sar with Rung default ON

  • A sar led with a rung (trump) card can never be picked up, except sar 13. The pile stays, the senior player keeps the lead, and there's no bar.
  • To collect the pile you must lead a plain (non-rung) suit.
  • The forced beat: when a rung card is led on a sar that could otherwise be picked up (sar 5 or later, not barred, not 13), the other team must beat the led rung card if they can.
    • It's team-aware: the first opponent may hold back only if their partner, still to play, has a bigger rung card.
    • Once someone beats the led card, nobody else is forced.
    • It never overrides follow-suit, and a demoted "Ace = 2" is never forced.

OFF: rung leads can win the sar like any other lead.

10. Other buttons

  • ↩ Change card (10s): take back a misplayed card within about 10 seconds, until the next card is played. This isn't available for a card demoted by the 🂡🂡 rule.
  • ⇄ Switch to Thulla (host, between games): change the room's game. The bot leaves automatically.
  • 🤖 Add a bot / ✕ on its chip (host): seat or remove Botta.

11. A worked example

Teams: Ali & Saad vs Bilal & Hamza. The rung is ♥. All switches ON.

  1. Sars 1–4: they're played and stay in the pile (4 sars). Ali heads sar 4, so he's senior and leads sar 5.
  2. Sar 5: Ali leads K♠. Bilal plays 7♠, Saad 3♠, Hamza 9♠. Nobody beat him, so Ali's team picks up all 5 sars. Score 5–0.
  3. Sar 6 is barred. Bilal cuts with 4♥ and heads it. It goes into a new pile (1 sar), and Bilal leads sar 7.
  4. Sar 7: Bilal leads A♣ and nobody beats it. Because of the 🂡 rule, an Ace can't win the sar: Bilal stays senior but the pile (now 2 sars) stays put.
  5. Sar 8: Bilal leads A♦. His last card was an Ace that stayed senior, so under the 🂡🂡 rule this A♦ counts as 2♦. Saad plays 5♦ and beats it, so Saad is now senior. The pile is 3 sars.
  6. Sar 9: Saad leads Q♥ (rung). Because of the 🎺 rule a rung-led sar can't be won, and Bilal and Hamza must beat the Q♥ if they can. Hamza plays A♥, so Hamza is senior. The pile is 4.
  7. Sar 10: Hamza leads J♠ and nobody beats it. Hamza's team takes the pile of 5 (sars 6–10). Score 5–5.
  8. Sar 11 is barred, and play continues until sar 13, whose header takes whatever is left.

12. Tips for new players

  • Count rung cards. Once the big ones are out, your medium rung cards become winners.
  • Don't beat your partner when they're about to collect. Overtaking them spoils the pickup.
  • With the 🂡 rule on, defend with a King rather than an Ace when you want to collect.
  • With the 🎺 rule on, leading rung is a safe way to keep the lead and pull out the other team's rung cards, but it never collects.
  • Talk on voice when your team wins: decide who picks the rung before tapping.

🎛 Room & buttons

Everything outside the card rules: joining, voice, chat, the host's controls and what happens if someone drops.

Joining a room

  • Create: enter your name, pick ♠ Thulla or 🎺 Rung, then tap Create a room.
  • Invite: tap the gold room code at the top to copy an invite link. Anyone who opens it joins automatically.
  • Join by code: type the 4-letter code and tap Join.
  • Joining mid-game? You watch as a spectator 👁 and get dealt in when the game ends.
  • Same name = same seat. If you get disconnected, rejoin with the same name to get your seat back.

Voice chat 🎧 🎙

  • Voice is opt-in. Tap 🎧 (right side, above the cards) to join. It turns blue when you're in. Tap it again to leave.
  • Allow the microphone when your browser asks.
  • Hold 🎙 to talk and let go to stop. On a computer, hold Space.
  • A green glow on someone's chip means they're talking.
  • Spectators can listen but not talk (👂).
  • If you switch to another app for more than a minute, voice turns itself off to save battery. Tap 🎧 to rejoin.

Chat 💬

Tap 💬 Chat for the room chat. Everyone, including spectators, sees every message. A red badge shows unread messages.

Host controls

The host is the first connected player in the seat order. If they leave, the badge moves to the next person.

Start game / Play againDeals a new game once enough players are seated (Thulla 3+, Rung 4).
⇄ Switch to …Changes the room between Thulla and Rung between games. Same code, chat and voice.

🛑 Dissolve gameTap twice to cancel the current game. Nothing is recorded and everyone keeps their seat.

If someone disconnects

  • Everyone sees a 30-second countdown. If the player is back in time, the game just continues.
  • If not, they're removed and a new game starts automatically with whoever is left (or the room goes back to the lobby if there are too few players).
  • Thulla players who are already safe can leave freely.

Scoreboard

Each player's chip shows 🎮 games, 🏆 wins and 💀 losses. The full standings appear on the game-over screen with the biggest loser 👑 on top. Stats survive reconnects but reset if you leave the room. Aborted games don't count.
